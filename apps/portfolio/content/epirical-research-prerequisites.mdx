# What is an Empirical Research (ER)?

At the very beginning of my application for the empirical research project of my **FYP**, my first impression for a project labelled with 'ER' was like an in-between concept combining the theoretical research and software engineering, which meant that the deliverables should include both a workable system and an insightful report (almost like a paper to be published on xxx conference!).

Well, this was a bit naive.

After I faced issues writing my project scheme for the proposal of an ER project, the supervisor for that project was extremely strict about that, since students who got accepted for his project last year all failed to conduct the empirical research, most of them only ended up with a specific software while did not propose a research problem and conduct experiment to solve it - on the other hand, the module convenor for the FYP did not draw out the topic - **WHAT EMPIRICAL RESEARCH IS**.

## The Framework: Everything is About Your Research Questions

Literally, everything you do should revolve around the research questions you’ve identified based on the project description - I admit this is hard, which means that you'll need to break down and brainstorm every detail from a short, few-line project brief.

Notice that the research questions you proposed should reflect on specific problems that have not been covered by existing research. For instance, you want to integrate GenAI for a certain function in a system in order to provide recommendations for users, because this could reduce trial-and-errors. At the same time, you should also perceive that the delays caused by the inference time might on the other hand affect user's experience.
